Parking guide

Rules and no-parking streets

What applies on Sunday, October 4, 2026

The short version

  • Every Town deck, lot and street space is free.
  • One-hour and two-hour limits are not in force on a Sunday, unless the sign states other days. Fifteen-minute zones are, except the arts center lot's, which is not enforced during performances.
  • A public street with no sign and no yellow curb is legal to park on, except in the places below that need no sign. Private roads and drives are not covered by the Town Code and may tow.
  • Accessible spaces are enforced at all hours. Where this guide calls a lot unrestricted, that means every space except the marked accessible ones.
  • Private lots tow. Stay out of them unless the owner says otherwise.

Good to know

Desk research only.
Nothing on this page was checked on the ground. The counts rest on an aerial image flown on February 14, 2025. The newest pictures found are street-level imagery of July 29, 2025 and a satellite image of April 18, 2026. Nothing shows the area as it is this autumn. Walk the site before sending the advisories; the list is below.
Street parking near the building ended on April 10, 2025.
Dry Avenue west of the front door, S Harrison Avenue and S Walker Street are now no parking at any time, and S Academy Street allows parking only in its marked stalls. The arts center's own parking page still describes street parking on Academy Street, Faculty Avenue and Park Street. The Town's own records disagree in places; see the rules table.
The school lots are public on a Sunday, but the tournament fills them.
The circular lot beside the fly tower, the angled row on the east drive, the back lot and the south row are Cary Elementary property: about 134 spaces, all nearer to the field than to anything else. Under a joint-use agreement of 2011 the Town has the use of them outside school hours, so arts center patrons may park there. They are free and open to the public whenever school is not in session, including all day on weekends; only marked accessible spaces are enforced at all hours. That does not keep a space free: the tournament arrives six hours before the audience. They are left out of the totals and shown as a figure of their own. No listing for the tournament was found, so its size is unknown and this page assumes it fills all of them. The field is one grass area of about 390 by 310 ft. A published traffic study puts a local tournament at about 60 cars for each full-size field, which gives roughly 60 to 150 cars here depending on how the field is divided. That is an estimate from the field's size, not a finding about this event. It is about what the school lots hold, so the tournament may spill little beyond them.
The Walnut Street deck can be closed to arriving cars.
Visitors report that attendants close the deck during big downtown events, sometimes with spaces still free, because spaces are held for residents of The Walker. The reports run from 2023 to April 2026 and are all from festival days or sold-out evenings. October 4 has park events but no festival. If the deck is closed, the Academy Street deck is the next choice. A third Town deck, on Cedar Street north of the railroad, opened in March 2026 with about 290 public spaces. It is about 2,000 ft away in a straight line, beyond the range of this page, and the walk to it was not measured.
The gravel lot beside the deck was kept.
Its 40 or so spaces, between the deck and Walnut Street, had to be improved or removed by mid-September 2026, under the Town's recorded agreement with the landowner. The Town reported the improvements complete in April 2026, and an image of April 18 shows the lot open and in use. It is now counted in the totals. The agreement lets an improved lot stay until building starts on the site. Nothing later than April confirms it, and the land is private, so look before naming it to anyone.
The loading dock is at the south-west corner.
Street-level imagery of July 29, 2025 shows a roll-up dock door at the foot of the fly tower, facing west, with a single door up a few steps beside it. It is reached through the arts center lot from Dry Avenue, and NO PARKING is painted on the pavement in front of it. That is the point this page measures to. No published source names the door the company uses, so confirm it.
Construction at Walnut Street and S Walker Street.
The houses on the block between Walnut Street, Byrum Street and S Walker Street came down under demolition permits issued on July 20, 2026 and signed off on September 22. The permits for the apartment buildings were still under review on September 15, so building has not begun. Draft traffic plans close a lane of Walnut Street and close S Walker Street to through traffic. No record shows them in force: the Town, the state road feed and Waze showed no closure on September 28. Either would slow the drive into the Walnut Street deck, so look again on the day.
Map apps will mislead some patrons.
Google Maps lists the arts center as closed on Sundays, and its directions lead to the front steps, not to the lot. Apple Maps and Bing do not list the Academy Street deck at all, so give its street address. The patron advisory covers all three.
Some streets could not be assessed.
Charlie Gaddy Lane, the drive to the Old Cary Library lot (L6), and the new townhouse streets north of E Park Street (Mayton View Lane, Park Station Lane, Zev Summit Lane and Flat Rail Trail) have no entry in the Town's schedule and were not counted. The Town's street records list the townhouse streets as private, apart from the short Town stretch of Mayton View Lane at E Park Street (S23), and the development records describe the new homes as having garages of their own. Do not direct anyone to these streets.

Rules that need no sign

These apply on every street in Cary, signed or not (Town Code Sec. 34-94 and 34-103, and N.C. G.S. 20-162).

  • On a sidewalk, in an intersection, or on a crosswalk.
  • In front of a private driveway, or blocking a curb ramp.
  • Within 25 ft of the intersection of curb lines at a corner, or within 15 ft of the property lines where there is no curb.
  • Within 15 ft of a fire hydrant or the entrance to a fire station, and in any fire lane.
  • Within 30 ft of a stop sign, a traffic signal or a flashing beacon.
  • Within 15 ft of the entrance to a theater, hotel, hospital or public building. The arts center's front door is one.
  • Within 15 ft of a mailbox, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, except on Sundays and federal holidays.
  • Double-parked, in a marked bike lane, or so that a car cannot pass in a marked travel lane.
  • In a marked accessible space without a placard or plate, at any hour.
  • Outside the marked spaces in a Town lot.
  • Within 50 ft of a railroad crossing or underpass.
  • Where the car would block traffic: opposite a street excavation or construction site, or on a narrow street where other vehicles could not get past (Sec. 34-94, 34-103(7)).
  • On a bridge or other elevated structure (Sec. 34-103(8)).

Where no sign is posted, park parallel to the curb and within 12 inches of it (Sec. 34-96). A car left on the street for 24 hours or more may be towed (G.S. 20-161(e)), and one left in a Town deck or lot for more than 24 hours counts as abandoned (Sec. 34-208). The Town publishes no plain-language page on unsigned streets; this reading is from the Code itself.
